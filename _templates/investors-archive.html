01The case

Why this and not an index fund.

Small private businesses trade at low multiples of earnings because they're hard to buy and harder to run. Most buyers can only fix one of those problems. We're set up to fix both.

Bought cheap
Businesses this size sell for a low single-digit multiple of earnings, often with the seller carrying part of the price. That's a yield most public assets don't offer, before we change anything.
Run cheaper
The back-office work we automate is usually a meaningful share of overhead in a company this size. Taking part of that out goes straight to cash flow. We've already done the same work for a paying client and for ourselves.
Held, not flipped
There's no fund term forcing a sale into a bad market. Distributions come from operating cash. If someone offers a price that beats owning it, we'll take it to the investors.
Operators with skin in it
Max and Ryan put their own money in alongside investors and don't draw a management fee on the first deal. The founders get paid when the company does.

03Risks

What can go wrong.

The memo has the full list. These are the ones we think about most.

  • Customers leave with the owner. We diligence this hard and structure seller notes so the seller has a reason to help with the handoff. It is still the main way a deal like this fails.
  • The automation saves less than expected. Our estimates come from logged hours, not guesses, but a real company will surprise us. We underwrite deals so they work with no savings at all.
  • Debt service in a slow year. Leverage amplifies both directions. We keep it moderate and favor seller financing over bank debt where we can.
  • Two founders. We're a small team and the first acquisition will take most of our time. The manager model exists so the business doesn't depend on us being on site.
  • Illiquidity. There's no market for these interests. Plan to hold for years.
