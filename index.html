01What we do

Buy. Rebuild. Hold.

The model is simple. Most of the work is in the middle step, and that's the part we're unusually good at.

1

Buy

Profitable, owner-operated companies, usually from a founder who is ready to step back. Small enough that institutional buyers ignore them. Established enough that the customers keep calling.

2

Rebuild

Ninety days of operations work. We map how the business actually runs, then automate the repetitive office work with AI tooling we've already built and deployed for paying clients.

3

Hold

No fund clock and no flip. Each company sits in its own entity under the holding company, with a manager running it day to day and our systems keeping the overhead down.

02Why it works

The margin is in the back office.

A small services company does the same two hundred office tasks every week. Quotes, scheduling, invoices, collections, reminders, reports. Until recently that work needed people. Now most of it doesn't.

The owner usually knows this. What they don't have is someone to build it, and most of the time they are also the person answering the phone. We buy the company and bring the builders with us.

On the other side of the deal, a generation of founders is retiring. Many of their businesses are too small for private equity and too complicated for a first-time buyer. That gap is where we shop. The full thesis covers what we look for and what we walk away from.

03The playbook

Ninety days after closing.

We don't rebrand, we don't fire the office, and we don't rip out software that works. We measure first, then automate the things that cost the most time. Read the full playbook, or see the first phase run on your business.

  1. Watch the business run

    Weeks 1 to 3

    We sit with the office staff and the field crew. Every recurring task gets written down with how long it takes and who does it. Nothing changes yet.

  2. Instrument it

    Weeks 3 to 5

    Bank feeds, the job calendar, the phone line, and the inbox get connected to one dashboard. The owner sees numbers they have never had before.

  3. Automate the top five

    Weeks 5 to 11

    The five tasks that eat the most hours get an agent. Quoting, invoicing and collections, scheduling and reminders, intake, and reporting are the usual suspects.

  4. Hand it to a manager

    Weeks 11 to 13

    A general manager, often someone already in the business, runs it with the new systems. We stay on as owners and keep the tooling current.
